Bingo

The TV is the caller, your phone is your card. Daub fast, shout first — and pay for a bogus bingo.

1The hook

Everybody already knows how to play, so the room is loud within thirty seconds: the TV calls a number with a cheeky nickname, phones light up, thumbs hunt, and somebody yells "wait, wait, WAIT". The button that says BINGO! is always there, always tempting, and a bogus press costs you a point and two calls of humiliation on the big screen — which is exactly what people will do to each other on purpose. Rounds escalate from a simple line to a three-numbers-per-call blackout, so the last round is a frantic daub-fest where the winner is whoever can read fastest, not whoever is luckiest.

2A round from a player's seat

Ana is playing with five friends. The TV says Round 1 of 3 — LINE with a five-dot row lit up on a little grid; her phone shows the same pattern and a fresh 5×5 card with the B-I-N-G-O letters across the top and FREE in the middle. A six-second countdown runs out and the TV booms B 7 — "Lucky seven". She has a 7 in the B column, taps it, it turns green with a satisfying thunk from her phone. The TV's board fills in 7 in pink.

Calls keep coming every six seconds. G 52 "Weeks in a year" — got it. I 29 "Rise and shine" — got it, and now her middle row reads 7 · 29 · FREE · 52 · ▢. The phone quietly notes 1 away — you need O 66; on the TV a little "1 AWAY" ticker shows her avatar next to Dee's. Bo, across the room, mashes BINGO! with nothing close to a line. The TV flashes a red BOGUS BINGO! — "Count again, Bo.", Bo's chip goes grey, his phone shows a locked button for two calls, and the room roars.

N 38 "Christmas cake" — Ana daubs it but it doesn't help her row. Four seconds. Three. O 66 "Clickety click". She taps 66, sees the row go solid, and hammers BINGO! before Dee can. The TV cuts to a gold BINGO! Ana — "Ana shouted first!" with her card blown up and the winning row outlined; +5. Cy's phone says Ana got there first — but your line is complete too with a green I had it too! button; he presses it and the TV adds him under "Also had it, +3". Dee's phone says You were 1 away · +2. Twelve seconds later the scoreboard slides in, then round 2 deals new cards with an X pattern and two numbers per call.

3Complete rules

Setup

  1. The game plays rounds rounds (default 3). Each round every player is dealt a fresh 5×5 card: column B holds 5 distinct numbers from 1–15, I from 16–30, N from 31–45 (4 numbers, centre square is FREE), G from 46–60, O from 61–75. Cards are dealt from state.rng and differ per player.
  2. Each round has a pattern. With the default pattern = classic, round r uses [line, x, blackout][(r − 1) mod 3]. Otherwise every round uses the chosen pattern.
    • line: any full row, column or diagonal (12 lines; FREE counts). 1 number per call. Round cap: 60 calls.
    • x: both diagonals (8 numbers + FREE). 2 numbers per call. Cap: 38 calls (the deck).
    • corners: the four corner squares. 2 numbers per call. Cap: 38 calls.
    • blackout: all 24 numbers. 3 numbers per call. Cap: 25 calls (the deck).
  3. Each round shuffles a fresh deck of 1–75 from state.rng.

Play

  1. Intro (8 s): the TV shows the round number, pattern and cadence; phones show the pattern and their new card. Nothing can be daubed yet.
  2. Calls: the first call happens the instant play starts, then one call every callSeconds (default 6). A call reveals the next 1, 2 or 3 numbers of the deck (per the pattern) with their letter and a caller nickname from the content pack. All called numbers stay visible on the TV board.
  3. Daubing: a player may daub any square on their own card whose number has been called (in any earlier call, not only the latest). Daubing an uncalled number is rejected silently by the server (the phone greys those squares out only if assist is on). Daubs cannot be undone. FREE is pre-daubed.
  4. Calling bingo: pressing BINGO! claims that the pattern is completely daubed (called-but-undaubed squares do not count).
    • Valid claim → the round ends immediately in favour of that player (the "winner"), +5 points. The TV shows their card with the winning squares highlighted for 12 s.
    • Invalid claim → bogus bingo: −1 point, the button is locked until 2 further calls have happened, the TV shows a 3-second taunt with the player's name. Daubing still works while locked.
  5. Also had it: during the 12-second bingo reveal, any other player whose pattern is fully daubed may press BINGO! for +3 ("shared bingo"). A bogus press here counts as a bogus bingo (−1) but with no lockout (the round is over anyway).
  6. Closeness points: when the round ends, every player who is neither the winner nor a shared bingo is scored by the fewest undaubed squares on any completion of the pattern: 1 away → +2, 2 away → +1, otherwise 0. For blackout, "away" is the count of undaubed numbers on the card.
  7. No bingo: if the call cap is reached with no valid claim, the round ends with no winner; closeness points still apply. The VIP's skip during play ends the round the same way.
  8. Between rounds: an 8-second scoreboard shows totals, this round's deltas and the next pattern. After the last round the game goes straight to the final standings.

Winning

  1. Highest total wins. Ties are broken by (a) most round wins, then (b) fewest bogus bingos; players still tied share the rank.
  2. Awards (only when earned): Full House (won a blackout round), Caller's Nightmare (most bogus bingos, at least 2), Quick Daub (lowest mean daub reaction time with at least 10 daubs), So Close (most rounds finished exactly 1 away without a bingo, at least 2).

Referee's notes

  • Late joiners spectate: they see the TV board mirrored on their phone and the "1 away" ticker, and get dealt in next game.
  • A disconnected player's card and daubs persist; they can reconnect and continue. Their card still earns closeness points at round end.
  • Pause freezes the call timer; daubs and BINGO! presses are ignored while paused.

6Data model

// games/bingo/server/types.ts
import { z } from '@partybox/game-sdk';
import type { GameStateBase } from '@partybox/game-sdk';

export const PHASES = ['intro', 'play', 'bingo', 'scoreboard', 'done'] as const;
export type PhaseId = (typeof PHASES)[number];

export type Pattern = 'line' | 'x' | 'corners' | 'blackout';
export type PatternSetting = 'classic' | Pattern;

export interface Settings {
  rounds: number;            // 1..5, default 3
  pattern: PatternSetting;   // default 'classic'
  callSeconds: number;       // 3..12, default 6
  assist: boolean;           // default false — glow called-but-undaubed cells on the phone
  spicy: boolean;            // default false — cheeky caller pack overrides
}

export interface RoundState {
  number: number;                       // 1-based
  pattern: Pattern;
  deck: number[];                       // 75 numbers shuffled at round start
  drawn: number;                        // deck.slice(0, drawn) has been called
  calls: number;                        // call counter (drawn / perCall, rounded up)
  calledAt: number[];                   // ms timestamp of each call (length === calls); for Quick Daub
  cards: Record<string, number[]>;      // playerId -> 25 numbers row-major, [12] === 0 (FREE)
  daubs: Record<string, number[]>;      // playerId -> sorted daubed indices (always contains 12)
  lockedUntilCall: Record<string, number>; // playerId -> 'bingo' ignored while round.calls < this
  winnerId: string | null;
  winningCells: number[] | null;        // the completed pattern cells (for the reveal)
  alsoBingo: string[];                  // shared-bingo claimants, in claim order
  falseCalls: Record<string, number>;   // this round
  points: Record<string, number>;       // this round's points (incl. −1s), applied to scores at round end
  endedBy: 'bingo' | 'cap' | 'skip' | null;
  toast: { kind: 'false'; playerId: string; line: string; untilCall: number } | null; // TV taunt, cleared by the next call
  winLine: string | null;               // chosen win/no-bingo sentence (drawn from rng at round end)
}

export interface State extends GameStateBase {
  settings: Settings;
  round: RoundState;
  scores: Record<string, number>;       // totals after completed rounds
  roundWins: Record<string, number>;
  totalFalse: Record<string, number>;
  oneAwayFinishes: Record<string, number>;
  blackoutWins: Record<string, number>;
  daubStats: Record<string, { count: number; totalMs: number }>; // reaction time = daub.now − calledAt[call that revealed it]
  history: { round: number; pattern: Pattern; winnerId: string | null; calls: number }[];
}

export const inputSchema = z.discriminatedUnion('type', [
  z.object({ type: z.literal('daub'), index: z.number().int().min(0).max(24) }),
  z.object({ type: z.literal('bingo') }),
]);
export type Input = z.infer<typeof inputSchema>;

export const INTRO_MS = 8_000;
export const BINGO_MS = 12_000;
export const SCOREBOARD_MS = 8_000;
export const TOAST_CALLS = 1;      // toast shown until the next call
export const LOCKOUT_CALLS = 2;
export const PER_CALL: Record<Pattern, number> = { line: 1, x: 2, corners: 2, blackout: 3 };
export const MAX_CALLS: Record<Pattern, number> = { line: 60, x: 38, corners: 38, blackout: 25 };
export const POINTS = { win: 5, shared: 3, oneAway: 2, twoAway: 1, bogus: -1 } as const;

ControllerView never includes other players' cards or daubs; the TV includes a card only in reveal (the winner's). Contract-test hints: hiddenFromController = other players' card numbers as strings is NOT viable (numbers collide with called numbers), so the config instead asserts view.card equals the requesting player's card and that view.daubs ⊆ own daubs; hiddenFromTv = [] (cards are not secret per se, just not shown).

7Reducer logic

Per-phase transition rules

Phase Event Rule
any player setConnected. Nothing else changes; cards persist.
any vip pause/resume applyVip shifts the deadline. Inputs/timers ignored while paused.
any vip end enterDone: totals as they stand (an unfinished round's points are discarded).
intro timer · skip enterPlay → first call immediately.
play input daub Ignore unless: player in state.players, index ≠ 12, not already daubed, card[index] ∈ called. Then add index (keep sorted), record reaction time (now − calledAt[callThatRevealed(card[index])]). No transition.
play input bingo Ignore if not a player or calls < lockedUntilCall[p]. If completedCells(card, daubs, pattern) is non-null → endRound('bingo', p, cells). Else: points[p] −= 1, falseCalls[p]++, totalFalse[p]++, lockedUntilCall[p] = calls + 2, toast = {…, line: pick(rng, FALSE_LINES)}.
play timer (current instance) If calls ≥ MAX_CALLS[pattern] or drawn ≥ 75 → endRound('cap'). Else nextCall: drawn = min(75, drawn + perCall), calls++, push now to calledAt, clear toast if calls ≥ toast.untilCall, then enterPhase(state, 'play', now, callSeconds × 1000) (new startedAt ⇒ a new timer instance; the old one is stale by construction).
play skip endRound('skip').
bingo input bingo Ignore if not a player, is the winner, or already in alsoBingo. If pattern complete → push to alsoBingo, points[p] = 3 (replaces any closeness points). Else points[p] −= 1, falseCalls[p]++ (no lockout).
bingo timer · skip commitRound (add points to scores, update wins/stats/history), then round.number < settings.rounds ? enterScoreboard : enterDone.
scoreboard timer · skip enterIntro(round + 1): deal cards, shuffle deck, reset round state.
done anything state unchanged.

Pseudocode

// server/index.ts
function init(ctx) {
  const settings = readSettings(ctx.settings);
  let s = { ...base(ctx), settings, scores: zeros(ctx.players), roundWins: {}, totalFalse: {}, oneAwayFinishes: {}, blackoutWins: {}, daubStats: {}, history: [], round: emptyRound() };
  return enterIntro(s, 1, ctx.now);
}

function reduce(state, event) {
  if (event.type === 'player') return setConnected(state, event);
  const vip = applyVip(state, event, { skip: skipCurrent, end: enterDone });
  if (vip) return vip;
  if (state.phase.paused) return state;
  switch (state.phase.id) {
    case 'intro':      return reduceIntro(state, event);
    case 'play':       return reducePlay(state, event);
    case 'bingo':      return reduceBingo(state, event);
    case 'scoreboard': return reduceScoreboard(state, event);
    default:           return state;
  }
}

function skipCurrent(s, now) {
  switch (s.phase.id) {
    case 'intro':      return enterPlay(s, now);
    case 'play':       return endRound(s, now, 'skip', null, null);
    case 'bingo':      return afterBingo(s, now);
    case 'scoreboard': return enterIntro(s, s.round.number + 1, now);
    default:           return s;
  }
}

// server/phases/intro.ts
export function enterIntro(s, number, now) {
  let rng = s.rng;
  const pattern = patternFor(s.settings, number);
  const [deck, r1] = shuffle(rng, range(1, 75)); rng = r1;
  const cards = {};
  for (const id of Object.keys(s.players).sort()) { const [card, r2] = dealCard(rng); cards[id] = card; rng = r2; }  // sorted ids ⇒ deterministic
  const daubs = mapValues(cards, () => [12]);
  const round = { number, pattern, deck, drawn: 0, calls: 0, calledAt: [], cards, daubs, lockedUntilCall: {}, winnerId: null, winningCells: null, alsoBingo: [], falseCalls: {}, points: {}, endedBy: null, toast: null, winLine: null };
  return enterPhase({ ...s, rng, round }, 'intro', now, INTRO_MS);
}
export function dealCard(rng) {           // column c draws 5 distinct numbers from c*15+1 .. c*15+15
  const card = new Array(25).fill(0); let r = rng;
  for (let c = 0; c < 5; c++) { const [col, r2] = shuffle(r, range(c * 15 + 1, c * 15 + 15)); r = r2; for (let row = 0; row < 5; row++) card[row * 5 + c] = col[row]; }
  card[12] = 0; return [card, r];
}
export function reduceIntro(s, e) { return isTimerFor(s, e) ? enterPlay(s, e.now) : s; }

// server/phases/play.ts
export function enterPlay(s, now) { return nextCall(s, now); }
function nextCall(s, now) {
  const r = s.round; const per = PER_CALL[r.pattern];
  const drawn = Math.min(75, r.drawn + per);
  const calls = r.calls + 1;
  const toast = r.toast && calls >= r.toast.untilCall ? null : r.toast;
  return enterPhase({ ...s, round: { ...r, drawn, calls, calledAt: [...r.calledAt, now], toast } }, 'play', now, s.settings.callSeconds * 1000);
}
export function reducePlay(s, e) {
  if (e.type === 'input') {
    const p = e.playerId; const r = s.round; if (!s.players[p] || !r.cards[p]) return s;
    if (e.input.type === 'daub') {
      const i = e.input.index; const n = r.cards[p][i];
      if (i === 12 || r.daubs[p].includes(i)) return s;
      const callIdx = callIndexOf(r, n);            // index in calledAt of the call that revealed n, or -1
      if (callIdx < 0) return s;                    // not called yet → silently ignored
      const daubs = { ...r.daubs, [p]: [...r.daubs[p], i].sort((a, b) => a - b) };
      const st = s.daubStats[p] ?? { count: 0, totalMs: 0 };
      const daubStats = { ...s.daubStats, [p]: { count: st.count + 1, totalMs: st.totalMs + Math.max(0, e.now - r.calledAt[callIdx]) } };
      return { ...s, daubStats, round: { ...r, daubs } };
    }
    // bingo
    if (r.calls < (r.lockedUntilCall[p] ?? 0)) return s;
    const cells = completedCells(r.cards[p], r.daubs[p], r.pattern);
    if (cells) return endRound(s, e.now, 'bingo', p, cells);
    const [line, rng] = pick(s.rng, FALSE_LINES);
    return { ...s, rng, totalFalse: bump(s.totalFalse, p), round: { ...r,
      points: { ...r.points, [p]: (r.points[p] ?? 0) + POINTS.bogus }, falseCalls: bump(r.falseCalls, p),
      lockedUntilCall: { ...r.lockedUntilCall, [p]: r.calls + LOCKOUT_CALLS },
      toast: { kind: 'false', playerId: p, line: line.replaceAll('{name}', s.players[p].name), untilCall: r.calls + TOAST_CALLS } } };
  }
  if (isTimerFor(s, e)) {
    const r = s.round;
    if (r.calls >= MAX_CALLS[r.pattern] || r.drawn >= 75) return endRound(s, e.now, 'cap', null, null);
    return nextCall(s, e.now);
  }
  return s;
}

// server/phases/bingo.ts
export function endRound(s, now, endedBy, winnerId, cells) {
  let r = { ...s.round, endedBy, winnerId, winningCells: cells };
  const points = { ...r.points };
  if (winnerId) points[winnerId] = (points[winnerId] ?? 0) + POINTS.win;
  for (const id of Object.keys(r.cards)) {                       // closeness for everyone else
    if (id === winnerId) continue;
    const away = cellsAway(r.cards[id], r.daubs[id], r.pattern); // min over completions of undaubed cells
    points[id] = (points[id] ?? 0) + (away === 1 ? POINTS.oneAway : away === 2 ? POINTS.twoAway : 0);
  }
  const [winLine, rng] = pick(s.rng, winnerId ? WIN_LINES : NO_BINGO_LINES);
  r = { ...r, points, winLine: winLine.replaceAll('{name}', winnerId ? s.players[winnerId].name : ''), toast: null };
  return enterPhase({ ...s, rng, round: r }, 'bingo', now, BINGO_MS);
}
export function reduceBingo(s, e) {
  if (e.type === 'input' && e.input.type === 'bingo') {
    const p = e.playerId; const r = s.round;
    if (!s.players[p] || !r.cards[p] || p === r.winnerId || r.alsoBingo.includes(p)) return s;
    if (completedCells(r.cards[p], r.daubs[p], r.pattern)) {
      const away = cellsAway(r.cards[p], r.daubs[p], r.pattern); // 0 here; closeness already granted was 0
      return { ...s, round: { ...r, alsoBingo: [...r.alsoBingo, p], points: { ...r.points, [p]: (r.points[p] ?? 0) + POINTS.shared } } };
    }
    return { ...s, totalFalse: bump(s.totalFalse, p), round: { ...r, falseCalls: bump(r.falseCalls, p), points: { ...r.points, [p]: (r.points[p] ?? 0) + POINTS.bogus } } };
  }
  if (isTimerFor(s, e)) return afterBingo(s, e.now);
  return s;
}
export function afterBingo(s, now) {
  const c = commitRound(s);                                       // scores += points; roundWins, blackoutWins, oneAwayFinishes, history
  return c.round.number < c.settings.rounds ? enterPhase(c, 'scoreboard', now, SCOREBOARD_MS) : enterDone(c, now);
}

// server/phases/scoreboard.ts
export function reduceScoreboard(s, e) { return isTimerFor(s, e) ? enterIntro(s, s.round.number + 1, e.now) : s; }
export function enterDone(s, now) { return enterPhase(s.phase.id === 'bingo' ? commitRound(s) : s, 'done', now, null); }

Note on enterDone via VIP end: a round in play is discarded (its points are not committed) — the rule in §3; from bingo the finished round is committed first.

Pattern helpers (server/patterns.ts)

export const LINES: number[][] = [ ...rows(5), ...cols(5), [0,6,12,18,24], [4,8,16,20,12] ];
export function completions(pattern: Pattern): number[][] {
  switch (pattern) { case 'line': return LINES; case 'x': return [[0,6,12,18,24,4,8,16,20]]; case 'corners': return [[0,4,20,24]]; case 'blackout': return [range(0,24)]; }
}
/** Undaubed count on the best completion (12 is always daubed). */
export function cellsAway(card, daubs, pattern): number {
  const d = new Set(daubs); d.add(12);
  return Math.min(...completions(pattern).map((cells) => cells.filter((i) => !d.has(i)).length));
}
export function completedCells(card, daubs, pattern): number[] | null {
  const d = new Set(daubs); d.add(12);
  return completions(pattern).find((cells) => cells.every((i) => d.has(i))) ?? null;
}
export function callIndexOf(round, n): number {                   // which call revealed n (−1 if uncalled)
  const pos = round.deck.indexOf(n); if (pos < 0 || pos >= round.drawn) return -1;
  return Math.floor(pos / PER_CALL[round.pattern]);
}

Where randomness is drawn

  • enterIntro: deck shuffle (one shuffle), then one shuffle per column per player, in sorted player-id order.
  • Bogus bingo: one pick for the taunt line. Round end: one pick for the win / no-bingo line.
  • Nothing else. Views and scoring draw no randomness.

bot.sampleInput

sampleInput(state, playerId, rng) {
  const r = state.round; const card = r.cards[playerId];
  if (!state.players[playerId] || !card) return null;
  if (state.phase.id === 'play') {
    if (completedCells(card, r.daubs[playerId], r.pattern) && r.calls >= (r.lockedUntilCall[playerId] ?? 0)) return { type: 'bingo' };
    const called = new Set(r.deck.slice(0, r.drawn));
    const todo = card.map((n, i) => i).filter((i) => i !== 12 && called.has(card[i]) && !r.daubs[playerId].includes(i));
    if (todo.length && rng.chance(0.7)) return { type: 'daub', index: rng.pick(todo) };
    if (rng.chance(0.02)) return { type: 'bingo' };            // the occasional bogus call keeps the taunt path covered
    return null;
  }
  if (state.phase.id === 'bingo') {
    if (playerId !== r.winnerId && !r.alsoBingo.includes(playerId) && completedCells(card, r.daubs[playerId], r.pattern)) return { type: 'bingo' };
    return null;
  }
  return null;
}

The sim's random strategy will also send daub with arbitrary indices and bingo at any time; both paths are ignore-or-penalise, never throw.

8Scoring

Formulas

Event Points When
Valid bingo (first) +5 Ends the round; counts as a round win.
Shared bingo ("I had it too!") +3 During the 12-second reveal; pattern must be fully daubed. Replaces closeness points (they would be 0 anyway).
1 away at round end +2 Fewest undaubed cells on any completion = 1.
2 away at round end +1 = 2.
Bogus bingo −1 each Any invalid claim in play or bingo. Totals may go negative.

total = Σ rounds (win + shared + closeness + bogus). Settings change nothing about the point values; pattern and rounds change how many points are on the table (≈5–8 per round).

Worked example — Ana, Bo, Cy, Dee · 3 rounds · classic

Round (pattern) Ana Bo Cy Dee Notes
1 (line) bogus −1, shared +3 = +2 win +5 1 away +2 2 away +1 Bo claims on call 17; Ana had pressed early on call 9, then had it too during the reveal.
totals 2 5 2 1
2 (x) 1 away +2 bogus ×2 −2, 2 away +1 = −1 3 away 0 win +5 Dee claims on call 24.
totals 4 4 2 6
3 (blackout) shared +3 1 away +2 win +5 2 away +1 Cy blacks out on call 22; Ana's card was also full and she pressed in the reveal.
totals 7 6 7 7

Tie-break: Ana, Cy and Dee all have 7. Round wins: Ana 0, Cy 1, Dee 1 → Cy and Dee ahead of Ana. Bogus calls: Cy 0, Dee 0 → still tied → they share rank 1; Ana is rank 3; Bo rank 4. 

ranking = players sorted by (score desc, roundWins desc, totalFalse asc); rank shared only when all three keys are equal
winnerIds = every player with rank 1                                    // ['p3', 'p4']

Implementation note: buildResults ranks on score alone, so results() builds the ranking itself with the composite key and passes it through (still using rank()'s shared-rank convention: "1, 1, 3, 4").

Awards

id Title Condition Example
full-house Full House Won a blackout round (most blackout wins; tie → lowest player id) Cy
callers-nightmare Caller's Nightmare Most bogus bingos, ≥ 2 Bo (2)
quick-daub Quick Daub Lowest totalMs / count with count ≥ 10 Dee (1.9 s)
so-close So Close Most rounds finished exactly 1 away without a bingo, ≥ 2 nobody in the example (each had one)

Awards are independent of rank and never affect scores. Ties on an award go to the lowest player id (deterministic, replay-safe).

10Edge cases

Case Behaviour
1 player Works as solitaire. Line rounds usually end by call ~42; the 60-call cap ends the rest ("no bingo", closeness applies). minPlayers = 1.
Min / max players Cards for 16 players ≈ 4 KB; the TV "1 away" ticker truncates to 8 avatars + "+n". Shared-bingo chips wrap.
Disconnect in intro Card already dealt; nothing lost.
Disconnect in play Daubs live in state; the phone dims its card and disables BINGO! until reconnected (banner). Calls continue — nobody is waited for. The card still earns closeness points.
Disconnect in bingo Cannot claim a shared bingo unless back within 12 s. Winner status is unaffected.
Disconnect in scoreboard/done Nothing to do; view on reconnect is current.
VIP leaves Engine reassigns VIP; the game never knows who the VIP is. No phase depends on the VIP.
Everyone idle Rounds run to their cap (line 60 calls × 6 s = 6 min; x 38; blackout 25) with no winner and 0 closeness (nobody daubed). Defaults worst case ≈ 14 min < estimatedMinutes × 3 = 30. Extreme settings (5 line rounds at 12 s) can exceed the bound — see §14.
Empty / duplicate submissions Duplicate daub → ignored. daub 12 → ignored. Uncalled number → ignored (assist off means the phone can't tell you it was rejected; the cell simply doesn't fill — the phone plays no submit cue because no view change arrives; see §11). Duplicate bingo in bingo phase → ignored.
Two players press BINGO! on the same call First input processed wins (+5); the second gets the shared-bingo button in the reveal (+3). Nobody is punished for phone latency.
Bogus bingo, then a valid one before the lockout lifts Ignored until 2 calls have passed — the lockout is the penalty. The phone shows the remaining calls.
Ties everywhere Final: score → round wins → fewest bogus → shared rank (possibly everyone rank 1 with 0 points). Awards: lowest player id.
Late joiners Spectators (engine). controllerView gives them card: null and the called list; bot.sampleInput returns null for them.
VIP skip mid-phase intro → play now. play → round ends, no winner, closeness applies. bingo → commit and continue. scoreboard → next round now.
VIP end mid-round Unfinished round discarded; totals from completed rounds; results() lists every player (0 for those with no completed round).
Spicy off (default) Only calls.json is used. Toggling spicy mid-game is impossible (settings are fixed at init).
Unspent timers A valid bingo mid-countdown abandons the play timer (stale by startedAt). Skips likewise. The bingo timer always runs its 12 s unless skipped.
Pause during play Deadline shifts; the TV shows the shell's paused curtain over the board; daubs are ignored (the phone disables the card).
Deck exhausted mid-call x/corners: call 38 reveals 1 number; blackout: exactly 25 calls. drawn is clamped to 75.
Player who never daubs but has a called line Not a bingo — "daubed" is the rule; their phone's closeness line stays silent until they daub.

12Accessibility & TV readability

  • Sizes at 1080p: current call 192 px, nickname 48 px, board numbers 28 px (the smallest TV text; the board is 15 columns × 60 px cells = 900 px wide, well inside overscan), "1 away" ticker names 36 px, toast body 36 px.
  • Colour is never alone: called cells are bold + filled; the current call also has a ring and a larger cell; daubed phone cells get a filled circle behind the number and (in the reveal) an outline; the pattern is shown as a dot-grid and named. Bogus toast has the ✕ word "BOGUS" not just red.
  • Colour-blind safety: accent (pink) vs accent-3 (green) vs accent-2 (yellow) are separated by lightness (L* ≈ 62 / 78 / 87), so called/daubed/current remain distinct in deuteranopia; the phone additionally uses fill vs outline.
  • Screen readers (controller): the card is a grid role with 25 gridcell buttons labelled "B 7, daubed" / "N 38, not daubed" / "FREE"; the call header is an aria-live="polite" region announcing "N 38, Christmas cake"; BINGO! is a button with aria-disabled and the lockout text in its accessible name ("Bingo, locked for 2 calls"); the banner is role="status".
  • Touch: 60 × 60 px cells with 7 px gaps (≥ 44 / ≥ 8); BINGO! button 64 px tall in the sticky footer; nothing else is tappable in play, so mis-taps are impossible.
  • Reduced motion: no call-card rise, no cell pop, reveal all-at-once, toast without scale.
  • Cadence accessibility: callSeconds up to 12 s and assist exist precisely for players who read slowly; the VIP can also pause.

SDK gaps (also appended to sdk-requests.md)

  1. Quiet timer mode. The shells' Timer turns red, scales and plays countdown in the last 5 s of every deadline. A 6-second call cadence would tick five of every six seconds. Request: an optional envelope field timer?: 'normal' | 'quiet' | 'hidden' (contract change → ADR) honoured by TvPlaying and the controller shell. Workaround until then: the game sets callSeconds default to 6 and accepts the noise in review builds, or the shell suppresses urgency when deadline − startedAt < 15 s (needs startedAt in the envelope, also a contract change).
  2. Game-triggered sound cues. GameClientModule.sounds exists but GameTvProps/GameControllerProps carry no way to play one. Request: a useSound() hook from @partybox/game-sdk/ui (context provided by both shells) returning (moment: string) => void that maps through clientModule.sounds. Needed for the call, bingo and bogus cues.
  3. Chip status for "locked out". PlayerStatus has no negative state; the design reuses waiting. Nice-to-have: a 'penalty' status with a distinct glyph.

14Open questions

  1. Closeness by daubs or by calls? Recommended: daubs (as specified) — idle players earn nothing and the phone's "1 away" line rewards attention. Alternative: by called numbers, which is kinder to slow readers but makes doing nothing profitable.
  2. Should a bogus bingo cost −1 or just the lockout? Recommended: −1 + lockout. The negative number on the TV is half the joke.
  3. Termination bound for extreme settings. 5 line rounds at 12 s with nobody daubing ≈ 60 min > 30. Recommended: keep the settings, keep estimatedMinutes = 10, and rely on the contract test using defaults + the listed variants (all under the bound); note in the README that the VIP can skip a dead round. Alternative: cap line rounds at 45 calls (solo line rounds would then end without bingo ~40 % of the time).
  4. New cards every round, or one card for the game? Recommended: new cards (as specified) — a bad card only hurts for one round and blackout needs a fresh deck anyway.
  5. Auto-daub setting for accessibility? Not specified. Recommended: no — assist + callSeconds = 12 covers it without making the phone play itself. Revisit after the accessibility pass (BL-008).
  6. Points per daub (Skillz-style)? Blackout Bingo pays 100 per correct daub + up to 75 for speed and −25 for a wrong tap, which keeps every player scoring every call. Recommended: no for v1 — it inflates scores into the hundreds, makes the scoreboard unreadable from the couch and rewards mashing; the Quick Daub award and closeness points cover the same instinct. Revisit as a daubPoints boolean if idle rounds feel dead in playtests.
  7. Multiple cards per player? Every commercial app sells 4–8 cards per player. Recommended: one card — 60 px cells only fit once on a phone, and one card keeps the bogus-bingo bluff honest. A “2 cards” setting could come later with 48 px cells.
  8. Hall rule: must claim before the next call? Halls void a “sleeper” bingo announced after the next number. Recommended: no — phone latency and kids; a claim is valid whenever the daubs are.
  9. Show the "1 away" ticker at all? It reveals who to heckle. Recommended: yes, default on, no setting (fewer settings, more shouting).

16Prior art & references

What other platforms do, and what this design takes or rejects from each. Researched 2026-09-15.

Platform What it does Taken / rejected here
Bingo Party: Host & Play (Android/iOS, TV + QR) One player is the Game Master: calls manually or auto at Slow/Medium/Fast; picks patterns (lines, X, four corners, diamond, blackout); toggles allow multiple winners, late joining, verify BINGO claims; “Single” mode (first valid bingo wins) vs “Progressive” mode (several patterns per session, ordered or auto-sorted by difficulty, fresh winner each stage). Projector view: full 1–75 master board, latest number as an animated ball, call history, active patterns. Auto-mark is a paid feature; 4–8 cards per player. Taken: the projector layout (board + latest ball + history + pattern) is essentially §5's play screen; “Progressive” = our classic escalation; “multiple winners” = the shared-bingo window, but time-boxed (12 s) rather than a toggle. Rejected: a human Game Master — PartyBox's VIP only skips/pauses; the server calls, so nobody is stuck hosting. Manual claim verification is replaced by server-side validation with a penalty.
Crowdpurr Bingo (events, projector + phones) Manual, automatic or 24/7 calling; players score points for every marked square and for bingos; live leaderboard on the presentation view; team and quiz modes; branding. Taken: everybody earns something each round (our closeness points, their per-square points). Rejected: per-square scoring as the main economy (see §14) and rounds that never end — a party round needs a shout and a stop.
Bingo Buddies (browser, 1–20 players) No login; randomised cards; tap to mark; a BINGO button with instant server verification; custom text squares; 2×2 to 5×5 grids. Taken: tap-to-mark + a big BINGO button with instant verification. Not now: text-square (“icebreaker”) bingo — a natural content-pack extension (prompts instead of numbers) once the numeric game ships.
Skillz Blackout Bingo (competitive mobile) Timed 1-card blackout; 100 points per correct daub + up to 75 speed bonus; −25 for a wrong tap; −100 for a false bingo; 1 000 per bingo with escalating bonuses; power-ups. Taken: a false bingo must cost something (ours: −1 + 2-call lockout), and reaction time matters (Quick Daub award). Rejected: hundreds-of-points economies and power-ups; the TV scoreboard needs single-digit deltas.
Bingo Blitz (social casino) Auto-daub optional; power-up meter charges after 3 daubs; multi-card multipliers; daubing duels. Rejected wholesale (monetisation loops), but it confirms that 3-per-call blackout rounds are readable when the latest numbers stay pinned at the top of the card — hence the phone's call header.
Bingo halls (etiquette guides, US state regulations) Callers pace 4–6 s between numbers; a bingo must be claimed before the next call (“sleeper” bingos are void); the game stops for verification; false calls are corrected politely; ties split the prize; hosts open with quick patterns and play 2–3 rounds with different patterns. Taken: the 6 s default cadence, three rounds with escalating patterns, ties handled by the shared window. Rejected: the sleeper rule (§14) and “stop everything to verify” — validation is instant, so the game only stops for the celebration.

Design decisions informed by the research

  • Default cadence 6 s sits at the top of the hall range; 3 s is the “Fast” preset every host app offers; 12 s exists for accessibility, not for hosts.
  • Escalating patterns (line → X → blackout) mirror “Progressive / difficulty-sorted” modes, which hosts consistently describe as the format that keeps a room for three rounds.
  • Multi-number calls in the harder rounds are this design's answer to a problem the apps solve by speeding the caller up: a Monte Carlo (2 000 runs per pattern) shows X needs a median 53–61 numbers and blackout 68–71 for 4–16 players, which at one number per 6 s would be 6–7 minutes a round; at 2–3 numbers per call they land at 2–3 minutes with an unchanged reading pace. Line needs a median 23–31 numbers (42 solo).
  • Server-validated claims with a visible penalty replace the “verify claim” toggle — the joke is only funny if the machine is the referee.
  • Assist (glow called numbers) is the free middle ground between the apps' paid auto-mark and hunting unaided; auto-mark itself is deliberately absent because marking is the game on a phone.
