Bingo

The TV calls the numbers, your phone is your card. Daub what you like — but press BINGO! and the caller stops while the whole room checks your card.

1The hook

Everybody already knows how to play, so the room is loud within thirty seconds: the TV calls a number with a cheeky nickname, thumbs hunt, somebody yells "wait, WAIT". The button that says BINGO! is always there. Press it and the caller stops: your card goes up on the TV for everyone to see — the winning line in green, every square you daubed that was never called in red. Get it right and the round is yours. Get it wrong and the whole room just watched exactly where you cheated, your bad daubs are wiped, and the caller clears their throat and carries on.

2A round from a player's seat

Ana is playing with five friends. The TV says Round 1 of 3 — LINE with a five-dot row lit up on a little grid; her phone shows the same pattern and a fresh 5×5 card with B-I-N-G-O across the top and FREE in the middle. Five seconds later the TV fills with B 7 — "Lucky seven" in giant yellow digits. She has a 7 in the B column; she taps it and it turns pink. Six seconds later: G 52 "Weeks in a year". Tap. I 29 "Rise and shine". Tap. Her middle row reads 7 · 29 · FREE · 52 · ▢.

The calls keep coming every six seconds; the TV shows only the current one, huge, with the last three in small boxes underneath. Ana taps what she hears and once taps the wrong square by mistake — she taps it again and it clears. Bo, across the room, slams BINGO! The caller stops. The TV becomes Bo's card: two green squares, five red ones with little ✕ marks, and the words NOT A BINGO — 5 daubs were never called. Card goes back to Bo. Everyone turns to look at Bo. Bo's phone shows the same card, wipes the red squares, and its button says Wait for the next number. Five seconds later the caller resumes: N 38 "Christmas cake".

O 66 "Clickety click". Ana taps 66, sees her row complete, and hits BINGO! The caller stops again — this time the TV goes green: BINGO! Ana wins round 1, her card big with the middle row glowing green and a tick on every square. The room cheers at her, not the TV. Ten seconds later the scoreboard shows "Ana — 1 round", then round 2 deals everyone a new card.

3Complete rules

Setup

  1. The game plays rounds rounds (default 3), all with the same pattern (default line). Each round every player is dealt a fresh 5×5 card: column B holds 5 distinct numbers from 1–15, I from 16–30, N from 31–45 (4 numbers, the centre is FREE), G from 46–60, O from 61–75. Cards come from state.rng and differ per player.
  2. Patterns: line — any full row, column or diagonal (12 lines; FREE counts). corners — the four corner squares. x — both diagonals. blackout — every square.
  3. Each round shuffles a fresh deck of 1–75 from state.rng.

Play

  1. Intro (5 s): the TV shows the round number and the pattern; phones show the pattern and their new card.
  2. Calls: the first number is called the instant play starts, then one every callSeconds (default 6). The TV shows the current letter and number, huge, with its caller nickname, and the previous three numbers small. Nothing else.
  3. Daubing is free: tap any square on your own card to daub it; tap again to un-daub. The server accepts every daub — called, uncalled, right or wrong. FREE is always daubed.
  4. Claiming: press BINGO!. The caller stops. The TV shows your card next to your name for everyone to check:
    • pattern squares you daubed whose numbers were called are green ✓;
    • every daubed square on the card whose number was never called is red ✕;
    • pattern squares you did not daub are outlined.
    The claim is valid when some completion of the pattern is entirely green.
  5. Valid → "BINGO! You win round n" for 10 s, then the next round (or the final standings).
  6. Invalid → "NOT A BINGO" for 5 s; your red squares are wiped; then the caller continues with the next number. You may claim again once that next number has been called. Other players cannot claim while a card is being checked; they can right after.
  7. Deck empty: after 75 calls with no valid claim the round ends with no winner.
  8. Between rounds: a 6-second scoreboard shows rounds won. After the last round the game goes to the final standings.

Winning

  1. One round won = 1 point. Most rounds won wins the game; ties share the rank. No other scoring.

Referee's notes

  • Late joiners spectate: they see the current call on their phone and get dealt in next game.
  • A disconnected player's card and daubs persist; they can reconnect and continue.
  • Pause freezes the caller (and a running check); daubs and claims are ignored while paused.
  • VIP skip: in play → the round ends with no winner; in a check → the next number now; elsewhere → move on.

6Data model

// games/bingo/server/types.ts
import { z } from '@partybox/game-sdk';
import type { GameStateBase } from '@partybox/game-sdk';

export const PHASES = ['intro', 'play', 'check', 'bingo', 'scoreboard', 'done'] as const;
export type Pattern = 'line' | 'corners' | 'x' | 'blackout';

export interface Settings {
  rounds: number;        // 1..5, default 3
  pattern: Pattern;      // default 'line'
  callSeconds: number;   // 3..12, default 6
  spicy: boolean;        // default false — cheeky caller phrases
}

export interface Claim {
  playerId: string;
  cells: number[];       // the best completion of the pattern (most green cells)
  green: number[];       // cells of that completion that are daubed AND called
  red: number[];         // every daubed cell on the card whose number was never called
  missing: number[];     // cells of that completion not daubed
  valid: boolean;
}

export interface RoundState {
  number: number;                       // 1-based
  deck: number[];                       // 75 numbers shuffled at round start
  drawn: number;                        // deck.slice(0, drawn) has been called; current call = deck[drawn − 1]
  cards: Record<string, number[]>;      // playerId → 25 numbers row-major, [12] === 0 (FREE)
  daubs: Record<string, number[]>;      // playerId → sorted daubed indices (never contains 12; FREE is implicit)
  claim: Claim | null;                  // the card on the TV (check: invalid; bingo: the winner's)
  waitForCall: Record<string, number>;  // playerId → may claim again once drawn ≥ this
  winnerId: string | null;
}

export interface State extends GameStateBase {
  settings: Settings;
  round: RoundState;
  wins: Record<string, number>;         // rounds won
  history: { round: number; winnerId: string | null; calls: number }[];
}

export const inputSchema = z.discriminatedUnion('type', [
  z.object({ type: z.literal('daub'), index: z.number().int().min(0).max(24) }),   // toggles
  z.object({ type: z.literal('bingo') }),
]);
export type Input = z.infer<typeof inputSchema>;

export const INTRO_MS = 5_000, CHECK_MS = 5_000, BINGO_MS = 10_000, SCOREBOARD_MS = 6_000, DECK = 75;

Per-phase transition rules

anyplayersetConnected. Cards and daubs persist.
anyvip pause/resumeapplyVip shifts the deadline. Inputs/timers ignored while paused.
anyvip endenterDone: rounds won as they stand.

Pseudocode

// server/index.ts
function init(ctx) {
  const settings = readSettings(ctx.settings);
  const s = { ...base(ctx), settings, wins: zeros(ctx.players), history: [], round: emptyRound() };
  return enterIntro(s, 1, ctx.now);
}
function reduce(state, event) {
  if (event.type === 'player') return setConnected(state, event);
  const vip = applyVip(state, event, { skip: skipCurrent, end: enterDone });
  if (vip) return vip;
  if (state.phase.paused) return state;
  switch (state.phase.id) {
    case 'intro':      return isTimerFor(state, event) ? nextCall(state, event.now) : state;
    case 'play':       return reducePlay(state, event);
    case 'check':      return reduceCheck(state, event);
    case 'bingo':      return isTimerFor(state, event) ? afterBingo(state, event.now) : state;
    case 'scoreboard': return isTimerFor(state, event) ? enterIntro(state, state.round.number + 1, event.now) : state;
    default:           return state;
  }
}
function skipCurrent(s, now) {
  switch (s.phase.id) {
    case 'intro':      return nextCall(s, now);
    case 'play':       return enterBingo(s, now, null, null);
    case 'check':      return nextCallOrEnd(s, now);
    case 'bingo':      return afterBingo(s, now);
    case 'scoreboard': return enterIntro(s, s.round.number + 1, now);
    default:           return s;
  }
}

// server/phases/intro.ts
export function enterIntro(s, number, now) {
  let rng = s.rng;
  const [deck, r1] = shuffle(rng, range(1, 75)); rng = r1;
  const cards = {}, daubs = {};
  for (const id of Object.keys(s.players).sort()) { const [card, r2] = dealCard(rng); cards[id] = card; daubs[id] = []; rng = r2; }
  const round = { number, deck, drawn: 0, cards, daubs, claim: null, waitForCall: {}, winnerId: null };
  return enterPhase({ ...s, rng, round }, 'intro', now, INTRO_MS);
}
export function dealCard(rng) {           // column c draws 5 distinct numbers from c*15+1 .. c*15+15
  const card = new Array(25).fill(0); let r = rng;
  for (let c = 0; c < 5; c++) { const [col, r2] = shuffle(r, range(c * 15 + 1, c * 15 + 15)); r = r2; for (let row = 0; row < 5; row++) card[row * 5 + c] = col[row]; }
  card[12] = 0; return [card, r];
}

// server/phases/play.ts
export function nextCall(s, now) {
  const r = s.round;
  return enterPhase({ ...s, round: { ...r, drawn: r.drawn + 1, claim: null } }, 'play', now, s.settings.callSeconds * 1000);
}
export function nextCallOrEnd(s, now) { return s.round.drawn >= DECK ? enterBingo(s, now, null, null) : nextCall(s, now); }
function toggleDaub(s, p, i) {
  const r = s.round; if (i === 12) return s;
  const has = r.daubs[p].includes(i);
  const mine = has ? r.daubs[p].filter((x) => x !== i) : [...r.daubs[p], i].sort((a, b) => a - b);
  return { ...s, round: { ...r, daubs: { ...r.daubs, [p]: mine } } };
}
export function reducePlay(s, e) {
  const r = s.round;
  if (e.type === 'input') {
    const p = e.playerId; if (!s.players[p] || !r.cards[p]) return s;
    if (e.input.type === 'daub') return toggleDaub(s, p, e.input.index);
    if (r.drawn < (r.waitForCall[p] ?? 0)) return s;                                 // "wait for the next number"
    const claim = evaluate(p, r.cards[p], r.daubs[p], r.deck.slice(0, r.drawn), s.settings.pattern);
    if (claim.valid) return enterBingo(s, e.now, p, claim);
    const cleaned = r.daubs[p].filter((i) => !claim.red.includes(i));              // "card goes back" without the bad daubs
    const round = { ...r, claim, daubs: { ...r.daubs, [p]: cleaned }, waitForCall: { ...r.waitForCall, [p]: r.drawn + 1 } };
    return enterPhase({ ...s, round }, 'check', e.now, CHECK_MS);
  }
  if (isTimerFor(s, e)) return nextCallOrEnd(s, e.now);
  return s;
}

// server/phases/check.ts
export function reduceCheck(s, e) {
  if (e.type === 'input' && e.input.type === 'daub' && s.players[e.playerId] && s.round.cards[e.playerId]) return toggleDaub(s, e.playerId, e.input.index);
  if (isTimerFor(s, e)) return nextCallOrEnd(s, e.now);
  return s;                                                                          // 'bingo' inputs ignored during a check
}

// server/patterns.ts
export function completions(pattern) { /* line: 12 arrays of 5; corners: [[0,4,20,24]]; x: [[0,6,12,18,24,4,8,16,20]]; blackout: [range(0,24)] */ }
export function evaluate(playerId, card, daubs, called, pattern) {
  const d = new Set([...daubs, 12]); const c = new Set([...called, 0]);              // FREE (0) counts as called
  const red = card.map((n, i) => i).filter((i) => d.has(i) && !c.has(card[i]));
  let best = null, bestScore = -1;
  for (const cells of completions(pattern)) {                                        // best = most green cells
    const green = cells.filter((i) => d.has(i) && c.has(card[i]));
    if (green.length > bestScore) { bestScore = green.length; best = { cells, green }; }
  }
  const missing = best.cells.filter((i) => !d.has(i));
  return { playerId, cells: best.cells, green: best.green, red, missing, valid: best.green.length === best.cells.length };
}

// server/phases/bingo.ts
export function enterBingo(s, now, winnerId, claim) {
  const wins = winnerId ? { ...s.wins, [winnerId]: (s.wins[winnerId] ?? 0) + 1 } : s.wins;
  const history = [...s.history, { round: s.round.number, winnerId, calls: s.round.drawn }];
  return enterPhase({ ...s, wins, history, round: { ...s.round, winnerId, claim } }, 'bingo', now, BINGO_MS);
}
export function afterBingo(s, now) {
  return s.round.number < s.settings.rounds ? enterPhase(s, 'scoreboard', now, SCOREBOARD_MS) : enterDone(s, now);
}
export function enterDone(s, now) { return enterPhase(s, 'done', now, null); }

// server/scoring.ts
export function results(state) { return state.phase.id === 'done' ? buildResults(state, state.wins) : null; }

VIP end during play or check discards the unfinished round (nobody wins it); from bingo the win is already recorded.

Where randomness is drawn

  • enterIntro: one shuffle for the deck, then one shuffle per column per player in sorted player-id order.
  • Nothing else. Caller phrases are looked up by number.

bot.sampleInput

sampleInput(state, playerId, rng) {
  const r = state.round; const card = r.cards[playerId];
  if (!state.players[playerId] || !card) return null;
  if (state.phase.id !== 'play' && state.phase.id !== 'check') return null;
  const called = new Set(r.deck.slice(0, r.drawn));
  const daubs = new Set(r.daubs[playerId]);
  const looksDone = completions(state.settings.pattern).some((cells) => cells.every((i) => i === 12 || daubs.has(i)));
  if (looksDone && state.phase.id === 'play' && r.drawn >= (r.waitForCall[playerId] ?? 0)) return { type: 'bingo' };   // claims whatever looks complete — sometimes wrongly
  const todo = card.map((n, i) => i).filter((i) => i !== 12 && called.has(card[i]) && !daubs.has(i));
  if (todo.length && rng.chance(0.7)) return { type: 'daub', index: rng.pick(todo) };
  if (!looksDone && rng.chance(0.05)) return { type: 'daub', index: rng.int(0, 24) };   // a stray tap now and then → red squares in checks
  return null;
}

The contract's random/fast strategies drive this bot with random timing (they do not invent inputs), so checks happen only when a card looks complete; after a failed check the reds are wiped and the bot cannot re-claim until the next number. The separate fuzz test sends arbitrary events, which the reducer ignores or evaluates without throwing. Verified in §13.

EventPoints
Valid bingo+1 round won. The round ends.
Anything else0. Invalid claims cost nothing but embarrassment and one number's wait.

callFor(n, spicy) = the spicy override if spicy and one exists for n, else the family call. The letter is derived ('BINGO'[⌊(n−1)/15⌋]), never stored.

Authoring guidelines

  • Calls are ≤ 5 words, read aloud in one breath. Rhymes and shape-of-the-digit jokes travel best; no brands, no people, nothing time-sensitive.
  • Family = fine for a ten-year-old. Spicy = innuendo and teasing, no profanity, no body-shaming.
  • Source: traditional British bingo-caller lingo (public-domain folk phrases), adapted to 75-ball numbering; a few are original where the traditional call referenced a brand.

10Edge cases

CaseBehaviour
1 playerWorks as solitaire (minPlayers = 1). Line rounds end around call 42 on average; the deck bounds it.
16 playersState ≈ 5 KB; nothing scales with player count except the shell's chips.
Disconnect in any phaseCard and daubs persist; calls continue — nobody is waited for. A disconnected player cannot claim.
VIP leavesEngine reassigns the VIP; the game never references it.
Everyone idleEach round runs the whole deck (75 × 6 s = 7.5 min) and ends with no winner; 3 rounds ≈ 23 min < estimatedMinutes × 3 = 30. Extreme settings (5 rounds at 12 s) exceed the bound — §14.
Empty / duplicate submissionsDuplicate daub toggles back — that is the undo. daub 12 → ignored. bingo during a check → ignored; right after your own failed check → ignored until the next number (the phone's button says so).
Claim spamOne check per claim, one claim per player per number after a failure, claims during checks ignored. Worst case with N spamming humans: N checks per number (N × 5 s). The game's own bots never spam (§7); see §13 for measured worst cases.
Two claims on the same numberFirst processed is checked; the second is ignored during the check and can be made right after (if the first was valid the round is over — first to press wins, as in a hall).
Valid pattern plus stray red daubs elsewhereStill a bingo — the pattern line is fully green. The reds show in the celebration for laughs.
Claim with an un-daubed pattern square that was calledInvalid — you must daub it. The square is outlined in the check so the player sees what they missed; they can daub it during the check and claim after the next number.
Ties everywhereRounds won tie → shared rank (everyone rank 1 when nobody won anything).
Late joinersSpectators (engine): card: null, they see the current call and the called list; bots return null for them.
VIP skip mid-phaseintro → play now. play → round ends, no winner. check → next number now. bingo/scoreboard → next.
VIP end mid-roundUnfinished round discarded; results() lists every player with their rounds won.
Spicy off (default)Family phrases only. Settings are fixed at init.
Unspent timersA claim mid-countdown abandons the play timer (stale by startedAt); the check's own timer then resumes the caller. Skips likewise.
Pause during a checkThe check's deadline shifts; the card stays on screen.
Deck empties during a checkThe check's timer calls nextCallOrEnd, which ends the round with no winner instead of drawing.
Blackout patternOne number per call, ~70 calls at 6 s ≈ 7 min a round. The intro text says so; hosts who want it fast set callSeconds to 3.

12Accessibility & TV readability

  • Sizes at 1080p: called number 300 px, nickname 48 px, previous calls 36 px, check-card numbers 48 px in 132 px cells, verdict 48 px. Nothing on the TV is below 28 px.
  • Colour is never alone: green cells carry ✓, red cells ✕, missing cells a dashed outline; the verdict is a word; daubed cells on the phone have a filled disc behind the number as well as the colour.
  • Colour-blind safety: green (#06d6a0) vs red (#ef476f) differ strongly in lightness (L* ≈ 78 vs 55) and carry different glyphs; daub-pink vs green differ by glyph (none vs ✓).
  • Screen readers: the card is a grid of 25 toggle buttons labelled "B 7, daubed" / "N 38, not daubed" / "FREE"; the call header is aria-live="polite" ("N 38, Christmas cake"); the check result is role="status" ("Not a bingo: 5 daubs were never called"); the disabled BINGO! button's name says why ("Bingo, wait for the next number").
  • Touch: 60 × 60 px cells with 7 px gaps; BINGO! 64 px tall in the sticky footer; nothing else is tappable.
  • Reduced motion: no rise, no pop, check squares at once.
  • Pace: callSeconds up to 12 s; the VIP can pause; the previous-three strip forgives a glance away; a check is 5 s of everyone catching their breath.

Design simulation (run before implementation)

docs/game-ideas/_tools/sim-001-bingo.mjs is a headless model of this exact reducer (cards, deck, toggling daubs, evaluate, the play/check loop, VIP skip/pause/end) driven by contract-shaped events and the bot above plus an idle and a hostile spam strategy. Run: node docs/game-ideas/_tools/sim-001-bingo.mjs. Results, 2026-09-15:

  • Unit checks: evaluate() 6/6 (valid line; stray red outside a valid line still valid; un-daubed called square → missing, invalid; uncalled daubs → red, invalid; blackout; corners). dealCard(): 2 000 cards, column ranges + uniqueness + FREE all pass.
  • Fuzz: 100 × 300 arbitrary events (inputs from spectators, unknown and empty ids, stale timers, VIP actions in every phase) — reduce, both views and the bot never threw.
  • Defaults (3 line rounds, 6 s), honest bot with random timing, 40 games of 1–16 players: median 9.8 min, p90 13.3, max 15.3 (bound 30). ≈3 checks per game (bots do press early sometimes), 3 valid bingos, no round ran out of deck. Same with the fast strategy. Replays are byte-identical.
  • Idle room (nobody daubs): 23.4 min = three full decks — under the bound, and the VIP can skip. VIP strategy (skip / pause / disconnects every 25 loops): max 12.7 min, 0 violations.
  • Contract variants: 1 blackout round at 3 s → median 3.9 min; 2 corners rounds at 3 s → median 4.9, max 8.0. X pattern at 6 s, 3 rounds: median 19 min, max 22.3 — legal but slow, which is why the intro text warns and 3 s is recommended for X/blackout.
  • Hostile spam (every player false-claims whenever allowed, random daubs): ≈100 checks per game; median 20 min at defaults, and with 12–16 spammers a game exceeds the bound — with N hostile players every number costs up to N × 5 s. This is the price of the friendly pause the owner chose; it needs N humans all trolling. §14 lists the one-line valve if the stress session wants it.
  • Invariants (after every event of every run): a winner's cells are all daubed and called; a check's red cells were never called and are wiped from the card; FREE is never in daubs; drawn ≤ 75; results list every player. 0 violations in ~900 games.
  • Sizes: largest state 4.2 KB (16 players); largest view 2.4 KB.
  • Harness lesson for the implementer: the contract's play loop caps time at deadline − 1 while a bot keeps returning inputs, so a bot that always has something to do starves the timer and the run is reported stuck. sampleInput must return null when there is nothing sensible to do (the bot above does; a first draft of the hostile bot did not).

SDK gaps (in sdk-requests.md)

  1. R-1 — quiet timer mode. The shells' Timer turns red, scales and plays countdown in the last 5 s of every deadline; a 6-second call cadence would tick five of every six seconds, and a 5 s check would tick throughout. Request an envelope field timer?: 'normal' | 'quiet' | 'hidden'.
  2. R-2 — game-triggered sound cues. Needed for the call sound; the check/bingo sounds can ride on phase entry via clientModule.sounds.

14Open questions

  1. Decided (owner, v0.3): free daubing; the TV shows only the current call; a claim pauses the caller and shows the card with green/red so the room experiences it together; wrong → card goes back; right → win. The earlier bogus-bingo penalty, lockout, shared-bingo window, closeness points, multi-number calls and awards are gone.
  2. Wipe the red daubs on an invalid claim, or leave them? Specified: wipe — "card goes back" clean, so the next claim is honest and the player does not have to hunt for their mistakes. Alternative: leave them and let the player fix it.
  3. "Wait for the next number" after a failed claim. Specified: yes — it is what a hall caller says, and it is the only thing standing between the game and a toddler holding the button. Alternative: no wait at all (checks would then chain back-to-back; the sim's spam strategy shows the cost).
  4. Safety valve against claim spam? The simulation's hostile strategy (every player false-claiming on every number) can push a 16-player game past the bound because each check pauses the room for 5 s. Recommended: ship without one — it needs a whole room trolling, and the "wait for the next number" rule already stops any single person. If the stress session wants a valve, the cheapest is one check per called number (further claims wait for the next number), which bounds a round to 11 s × 75 regardless of player count.
  5. Termination bound for extreme settings. 5 rounds at 12 s with nobody claiming ≈ 75 min > 30. Recommended: keep the settings, rely on the contract test using defaults + the listed variants (both under the bound), and note in the README that the VIP can skip a dead round.
  6. Show the previous three calls on the TV? Specified: yes, small. Strictly "just the current number" would punish a glance at the phone; three faded boxes cost nothing. Easy to remove.
  7. Escalating patterns per round (line → X → blackout)? Dropped for simplicity; pattern applies to every round. Could return as a fifth select value "classic" later.

What other platforms do, and what this design takes or rejects from each. Researched 2026-09-15.

Design decisions informed by the research

  • Default cadence 6 s sits at the top of the hall range; 3 s is the "Fast" preset every host app offers; 12 s exists for accessibility.
  • Every app auto-marks or validates daubs; this design deliberately does neither, because the owner wants the verification moment on the TV to carry the game. Free daubing is what makes the red squares possible.
  • Halls stop everything to verify a claim; so does this design — the owner's call, and the research says it is how bingo has always felt.
  • Monte Carlo (2 000 runs per pattern): first line among 4–16 players at a median 23–31 numbers (42 solo); corners 37–50; X 53–61; blackout 68–71. At 6 s per call that is ≈ 3 min for a line round and ≈ 7 min for blackout, which is why line is the default and blackout is a choice.
