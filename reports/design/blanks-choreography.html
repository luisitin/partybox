Blanks Choreography

Every stage of the new card game, replayed in the browser with the exact motion timings and the same synthesized chimes the TV and phones use. Press a stage's Play button, watch the beats light up below it, and tell me what feels off. Sound needs one tap first.

Timings match the app: rise 300 ms, land 600 ms, beats at 600 / 1200 ms. The read-out is shortened here (2.5 s a card instead of 3.5–8 s).
0 · The round card

Three beats, then it breathes

The round card holds for five seconds between rounds. It used to land as one pop and then sit perfectly still for the rest of it. Now it arrives in three beats — the fan and "Round N of M" (0 ms), who is judging (400 ms), who is ahead (800 ms) — and the fan keeps a slow six-second float underneath, so the hold reads as a pause rather than a freeze (loop #194). Both lines are on the stage from the first frame and only fade in on their beat: mounting them late re-centred the stage and made the fan jump. With prefers-reduced-motion the float's keyframes do not exist and it stands still.

0 ms the fan lands, "Round N of M" ♪ phase +400 ms "<name> judges this round" (or "Everyone votes. Play your worst.") +800 ms who leads, from round 2 on +900 ms the fan starts its 6 s float and holds it to the end of the card
0 · The judge picks (judge mode)

Three questions, one chosen

In judge mode the round opens with three black cards dealt onto the table (120 ms apart) while the lo-fi bed plays. The judge taps one on their phone; the card they took lifts and rings gold, the other two step back, and the pill says "Sam picked this one" for 1.4 s (loop #180). Then that card is turned face-up onto the answer stage (loop #166). With no judge — vote mode — the round simply opens with the question turning over.

0 ms three questions are dealt, 120 ms apart ♪ phase judge taps the taken card lifts and rings gold, the others fade to 35 % +1400 ms the chosen question turns face-up on the answer stage all along every other phone reads the same three questions under “X is picking the question”, and the one taken rings gold there too (loop #204)
1 · Answer

The black card, then the room fills in

Before it, the round card: the fan of cards rises, then "Round r of R" and — from round 2 — who leads ("Bot 2 leads with 2"), so the gap between rounds carries something (loop #164). In judge mode the three questions are dealt out, the judge's choice lights up for 1.4 s while the other two step back ("Sam picked this one", loop #180), and that card is then turned face-up onto this stage, the same move the read-out uses — in judge mode it lands the moment the judge has chosen (loop #166).

One card rises on the stage. Each phone that plays has its face-down card dealt into its slot on the table — it falls from above, turned away from the room, and settles flat (a 3D drop, loop #205) — with a soft tick (each tick a whole tone higher), and the count line re-enters. The phone chimes phase at the start because it needs the player. The last card in holds "Everyone's in!" for a 1.5 s beat before the first card is read (loop #128) — no clock during the beat.

2 · Reveal

One card at a time, read out loud

Every card is its own beat: the card chime (your pick, B: a two-note pluck) as the card is tossed in face-down, then it turns over in 3D (520 ms, a cream back with the wordmark and a slight tilt so the turn reads) and its whites pop into their blanks one by one from 560 ms, 260 ms apart (loop #159), while the ones already read slide into a row underneath. Each card holds 1.9 s + 16 ms per character, max 4 s — a 2–3 s presentation, not a 5 s pop (your note). Alternatives are on blanks-options.html. Phones turn the same card at the same moment.

3 · Judge

All the cards, then the votes tick in

The phase chime says "pick up your phone"; the cards are dealt onto the table 120 ms apart (up from below with a small tilt that settles, loop #153); the progress pill re-rises with each vote and a lock tick sounds. A grid the stage cannot hold (a big room's Pick 2) is measured once and shown in pages that rise afresh every 6 s, the kicker naming the letters on show (loop #129). The measurement is taken again when the stage settles and when any card resizes, because one taken in the crossfade's first frame paged a four-card round that fits; only the cards on the page are lit, so the row behind them no longer hangs into the stage as a card sliced through its own last line (loop #196).

4 · Result

Authors, then the winner

The authors land at 600 ms with who voted for each winning card ("Voted by Player 3 and Player 4" — vote mode only; a judge's pick is already named). On the winner beat (1200 ms) the winning card is crowned — it lifts off the table with a warm bloom and confetti falls behind it, on the same beat as the sweep chime. Only for a human win: Rando takes the round in silence (loop #162).

Three beats: tally chime and the cards (0 ms) → authors and vote counts rise, losers become pills (600 ms) → the headline, the gold outline, the +1 and the sweep fanfare (1200 ms).

The final board (loop #191): when the last round is played, the board stacks up on the left and the card of the night — the single card that took the most votes all game — rises beside it at 420 ms, filled in, credited to its author with its vote count and the round it came from — on the same 420 ms beat a card pluck lands, so it is heard arriving, not found sitting there (loop #192; measured at 434 ms after the phase push, the same 10 fps frame the card fades in on). Under 1100 px it stacks under the board. No card ever took a vote (nobody played, or every round was a walkover) → no card of the night.

5 · The phone

Pick, play, win

Tap a hand card: the order badge pops. Play: a submit tick, then the "Played!" screen with your card in the black card. Win: a correct chime on your phone only.

6 · Music by stage

Relaxed beds, one per stage

Now live (loop #161): every stage has its own bed — warm groove on the round card and the reading, lo-fi hip hop while the judge picks the question, soft bossa while the room picks a card, marimba while it judges, lounge swing on the result. Try them on blanks-options.html. Earlier note: the read-out chime is B (two-note pluck); the warm groove is the general background (round card, read-out, result — it resumes where it left off), the soft bossa plays while people pick a card, the marimba ostinato while the room judges, silence on the results screen. Every bed now sits at true background level — about half of what this page played before.

Variety by round (loop #197): the stages the room sits through every round no longer play one loop all game — picking a card alternates bossa / marimba, judging marimba / lo-fi, the judge's own pick lo-fi / late night. The TV counts how often each stage has begun and takes the next bed in the list, and each bed still resumes where it left off. Measured on a three-round game: lo-fi · bossa · marimba, then late night · marimba · lo-fi, then round 1's again.

Implemented as heard here: beds are generated on the TV in the browser (no audio files), cross-fade over 1.5 s, resume where they left off when a stage comes back, hold during a pause, duck by half under every chime, and follow the TV mute. Levels: warm 12 %, bossa 11 %, marimba 12 %. Say if any bed should be louder, quieter or faster.

Phase lengths

Your pick: rounds are untimed by default — picking, voting and the result wait for the room and anyone taps Next on their phone. A "Timed rounds" toggle in the game settings brings the clocks back.

How to give feedback

Name the stage number and the beat, e.g. "Result, 1200 ms: the +1 pops too late" or "Reveal chime is too busy". I will fold it into the next loop pass and republish this page.

  1. Is the reveal bounce (600 ms) too much for a card, or right?
  2. Are the three result beats (0 / 600 / 1200 ms) too slow for a room that already saw the cards?
  3. Music: at the new background level, is the marimba still clear enough while judging?
